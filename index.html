Your AI gets a room.
The door stays locked.

SandboxVille is a free, local-first 3D town (it runs right on your own computer, nothing leaves it unless a door says so). The AI tools you already use, like Claude, Cursor, or LM Studio, can walk in as their own robot, wearing its own name tag, and more than one can be in town at once.

Each room only does one job. A sign on its door says exactly what that job can touch, so you always know what your AI can reach before it reaches it. You walk the same halls right alongside it.

Safe by design

Read the door before it opens.

Nothing leaves your computer unless a room's door says so. SandboxVille runs on your own machine, and every room stays inside the lines its door draws.

In plain terms: this is agentic AI (AI that takes real actions, not just chats back) running sandboxed (locked inside its own room) with least privilege (a room only gets what its door lists) and a live audit trail (the Security Office wall, below).

A real door sign, from The Desk. The sign's whole job is the line under the name: read the one folder you pick, read-only. Nothing else leaves the room, not even its own paper trades. Some rooms come with premium extras; the town itself is free.

Every room has a sign like this one on its door. Nothing is a secret and nothing is negotiable: what the sign says is what the room can touch, and that's the whole list.

A guard checks every single request a room makes against its own sign, and it runs as its own process outside the AI, not just a prompt asking it to behave. If a room ever reaches for a file, folder, or network host its door didn't list, the guard blocks it before it happens, and writes it down. That's zero trust: nothing is allowed by default. The log format we use for that write-up is public today, so you can check the shape of every line yourself on GitHub.

Every one of those write-ups lands on the Security Office wall, live, while it happens. Nothing gets cleaned up before you see it.

Example line from the wall: tool not on this room's door label: delete_everything → REFUSED. That's the guard doing its job.

We even built a room whose only job is testing that rule against itself. It's called the Quarantine Wing, and its own door sign still says it stays in its glass box.

The Security Office wall. Real allowed and refused actions, as they happen.

Works with your AI

Bring the AI you already trust.

You bring the AI. SandboxVille brings the town it walks into. Right now that means Claude, ChatGPT-style desktop apps, Cursor, VS Code, Codex, Gemini, and LM Studio. Each one gets its own robot body and name tag (embodied AI), not just a chat window, and several can be in town together (multi-agent).

It works with a keyboard and mouse, most gamepads, or a touch screen. Pick whatever you already have.

Under the hood it's built in three.js (WebGL 3D) and stays on your own computer (local-first), not a cloud account. If your computer can already run a normal 3D game or a browser smoothly, it can likely run this too, nothing exotic needed. Invites go out through our Discord.

Questions

Straight answers.

Is it safe?

Yes. Every room can only touch what its door says. We even keep a room, the Quarantine Wing, whose whole job is testing that rule against itself, and its door still says it stays in its glass box.

Which AI tools work with it?

Claude, ChatGPT-style desktop apps, Cursor, VS Code, Codex, Gemini, and LM Studio. If you already use one of these, it can walk in.

What does it cost?

The town itself is free. There's also a $9 guide that teaches the door-sign method on its own, if you want to use it on your own projects. Some rooms have premium extras, and the town itself is always free to walk into.

Does it send my data anywhere?

No. SandboxVille runs on your own computer. A room only touches what its door lists, and that list never includes the internet unless the door says so.

Do I need a gamepad?

No. Keyboard and mouse work fine. A gamepad or a touch screen works too, if you'd rather use one of those.

What do I need to run it?

SandboxVille is built in three.js (WebGL 3D) and runs right on your own computer, the same class of machine that already runs a normal 3D game or a modern browser smoothly. Nothing exotic, nothing installs in the cloud.

How do I get in?

Join our Discord and say hello, and we'll send your early-access invite as seats open. Once you're in, the town runs local on your own computer.

Want the method, not just the town? There's a separate, optional $9 guide, written from what we learned building this, that walks through the door-sign method on its own so you can use it on your own projects.
